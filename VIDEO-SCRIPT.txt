[0:00 — OPEN]
Hi — I want to walk you through what I found in the Customer Inbox Triage tool
and what I changed.

[0:10 — WHAT IT'S FOR]
The tool exists to do one thing: read every incoming customer message and
instantly decide how urgent it is, what it's about, and what the agent should do
next — so small teams can handle more volume without hiring.

[0:25 — WHAT I DISCOVERED]
When I tested it, the prioritization was backwards. It judged urgency by
punctuation and message length, not meaning. So "Server down now" — a real
emergency — got marked LOW and buried, while a cheerful "Thank you!" got marked
HIGH and jumped the queue. Categories also changed run to run, some recommended
actions were just wrong, and when the AI failed, the tool quietly showed a fake
answer as if it were real.

[0:50 — WHAT I FIXED]
I went after the highest-impact problem first: urgency. I rebuilt it to read
meaning — signals like "outage," "can't access," or "payment failed" now drive
priority. I locked categories to a consistent set, flagged urgent tickets for
escalation, and made the tool honest when the AI is down.

[1:15 — USER + BUSINESS VALUE]
For users, real fires get seen first and agents stop wasting time on the wrong
tickets. For the business, it protects the core promise: fewer missed
emergencies, fewer breached response times, more throughput per agent — without
new headcount.

[1:35 — TRADEOFF / LIMITATION]
One honest limitation: my urgency engine is keyword-based. It's fast, free, and
predictable — but a truly urgent message worded in a way I didn't anticipate
could still slip through as low priority. I built the rules to be easy to expand
so we can close those gaps quickly.

[1:50 — WHAT'S NEXT]
Next, I'd let the AI itself score urgency so it generalizes beyond keywords, add
per-customer tuning, and move the API key to a secure backend for production.
Thanks.
